## Measured ground truth (before any idea)

- Loop: ROUNDS 8, HAND_SIZE 7, MAX_PLAY 3, 4 plays per round = 32 plays per run.
- The verb is identical in round 8 and round 1: pick up to 3 of 7, order them, press Play.
- FIGURES: 5 shapes (MONOGRAM, CHAIN, COLUMN, STAIR, PAIR). FIG_PAY is a flat
  lookup by (figure, words bound). A fifth COLUMN pays exactly what the first
  paid: chips 150, mult 3. No mastery curve anywhere in the file.
- ORDEALS: 9 defined, 3 fire per SCHOLAR run (rounds 4, 6, 8), 1 per APPRENTICE
  run (round 8). So 6 of the 9 Ordeals are never seen in a given run, and
  APPRENTICE sees 1 of 9.
- LENS_SLOTS 5. Lenses are the only thing that escalates that the player chooses.
- APPRENTICE win rate 49.3% against a 15-30% roguelite norm.

- insight: EVERY escalating quantity in Overtone is a number the player does not
  touch (the target, the multiplier). EVERY quantity the player DOES touch
  (which three, in which order, for which figure) is the same in round 8 as in
  round 1. That is the stagnancy, stated exactly.
- insight: 6 of 9 Ordeals are unseen per run and APPRENTICE sees one. The game
  already OWNS its structural variety and spends almost none of it.

## technique — Causal Loop Mapping

- insight: The run has exactly one feedback loop: clear round -> buy Lens -> bigger
  multiplier -> clear round. It is a pure positive loop with no second input, so
  once it is running the player is a spectator and when it is not they are dead.
- insight: Nothing the player LEARNS feeds back into the run. Skill has no
  accumulator. A player who has spotted four CHAINs is in the identical mechanical
  position to one who has spotted none.
- idea: Add a second accumulator the player drives with play rather than money -
  a figure ledger. Every figure you complete is remembered for the run and the
  next one of that shape pays more.
- idea: Make the ledger visible as a row of five figure marks that fill in. A
  progress bar the player writes with their own hands.
- idea: Give the ledger a cost as well as a gain so it is a decision: repeating
  a figure levels it, but a NEW figure pays a one-off bonus. Breadth vs depth,
  every single hand.
- idea: Second loop candidate - the discard pile feeds something. Discards are
  currently a pure sink, information destroyed.
- idea: Second loop candidate - words you have played become unavailable, so the
  deck thins toward your worst cards and forces new shapes late.
- idea: Second loop candidate - the round's Demand reacts to what you played
  last hand, so repeating yourself pays less.
- insight: A roguelite loop needs a THREAT that also escalates from player
  action. Overtone's only threat is the target, and the target is on rails.
- idea: The Bookseller could raise the next target if you overshoot this one -
  greed is punished, so overshooting becomes a decision instead of a freebie.
- idea: Overshoot could instead bank into money, making the size of the clear
  matter. Right now clearing by 1 and clearing by 40,000 are the same event.
- insight: Clearing by 40,000 and clearing by 1 being identical is the single
  largest dead signal in the game. Every hand after the target is met is
  ceremony.

## technique — Assumption Reversal

- idea: REVERSE "the hand refills to 7 every play". What if the hand only refills
  at the end of a round? Then a round is one hand of 7+ managed across 4 plays,
  and every card spent is a card gone. Instant escalation inside a round.
- idea: REVERSE "you play up to 3". What if round 1 lets you play 1, and the
  cap RISES as the run goes - 1, 2, 3, then 4? The verb itself grows.
- idea: REVERSE "more cards is better". A cap that rises means early rounds are
  tight and readable and late rounds are combinatorially rich. The learning
  curve and the difficulty curve finally point the same way.
- idea: REVERSE "the target is fixed when the round starts". A target that
  climbs while you play - every hand raises the bar for the next - turns four
  plays into a race rather than four withdrawals from the same bank.
- idea: REVERSE "figures are found". Let the round NAME a figure it wants, and
  pay double for it. Now you hunt a specific shape rather than accepting
  whatever falls out.
- idea: REVERSE "ordeals are scheduled". Let the player CHOOSE their ordeal at
  the shop in exchange for money or a Lens. Voluntary difficulty is the oldest
  roguelite engine there is.
- idea: REVERSE "the Bookseller sells". Let the Bookseller BUY - sell him a Lens
  you outgrew for money, so the five slots become a live decision instead of a
  filling queue.
- idea: REVERSE "you keep your deck". A round that takes three random words out
  of your deck permanently would make the deck a resource that depletes.
- idea: REVERSE "all 8 rounds are played". Let a big overshoot SKIP the next
  round and take its reward - a risk/greed lever with real teeth.
- idea: REVERSE "words are neutral". Words the player has already played this
  run get tired and pay less - forcing rotation through the deck.
- idea: REVERSE "the player picks order". A figure that requires a specific
  order the round dictates (descending only, this round) is an Ordeal that
  changes the DECISION rather than removing a tool.
- idea: REVERSE "discards are free information loss". Discarding could bank a
  small charge that buys a re-deal or a peek later.
- insight: The strongest reversals all do the same thing - they make a resource
  that is currently infinite (cards, order freedom, target stability) finite.
  Stagnancy is what an infinite resource feels like.

## technique — Trait Transfer (graft from games that solved this exact problem)

- insight: Balatro has the identical skeleton - pick 5 of 8, 3 hands per blind,
  jokers multiply. It does NOT feel stagnant. The difference is worth naming
  precisely rather than admiring.
- idea: Balatro trait 1 - HAND LEVELS. Every poker hand type has a level; playing
  a Flush levels Flush, permanently, for the run. This is exactly figure
  levelling and it is the single most-cited reason Balatro's loop has a spine.
- idea: Balatro trait 2 - the blind is CHOSEN. Small/Big/Boss, and you may SKIP
  small or big for a tag reward. Choice before the round, not just after it.
- idea: Balatro trait 3 - the Boss Blind is REVEALED before you shop, so the
  shop is a decision about the specific wall ahead. Overtone names its Ordeal
  already; it does not let you buy against it.
- idea: Slay the Spire trait - the MAP. You choose your route and therefore your
  risk. Overtone's 8 rounds are a corridor.
- idea: Route candidate for Overtone - after each round, two doors: a harder
  target for a better Lens, or an easier one for money. Eight binary choices is
  a run you authored.
- idea: Into the Breach trait - PERFECT INFORMATION, short puzzle, no randomness
  to hide behind. Overtone could show the next hand's top card, making a play a
  plan rather than a reaction.
- idea: Luck be a Landlord trait - the deck SHRINKS as a choice. Removal is a
  reward. Overtone has no removal at all, so the deck only ever gets noisier.
- idea: Deck removal for Overtone - the Bookseller offers to burn a word. In a
  game about finding shapes, cutting your worst word is the most legible upgrade
  there is.
- idea: Dicey Dungeons trait - the same tools, radically different RULES per
  episode. Overtone's 9 Ordeals are exactly this and it fires 3 of them.
- idea: Slay the Spire trait - the rare card that changes how you play, not how
  much you score. Overtone's Lenses are almost all multipliers; none of them
  changes the verb.
- idea: Verb-changing Lens candidates - play 4 words; see 9 cards; reorder after
  seeing the score; play the same word twice; lock one card across hands.
- insight: Every game named here escalates the DECISION, not just the number.
  Balatro levels the hand, Spire branches the route, Dicey changes the rule.
  Overtone escalates only the number. That is the whole diagnosis in one line.

## measured baseline, 400 runs, SCHOLAR, optimal play, buy=value

- Win rate 23.5% (healthy band is 15-30%). Round-8 row reads 39.3% but that row
  counts WINS as well as deaths, so round 8 actually kills 39.3 - 23.5 = 15.8%.
  (This exact misread cost two wasted candidate curves in the previous session.)
- Deaths: r1 0.0 r2 1.8 r3 4.8 r4 13.8 r5 12.3 r6 17.0 r7 11.3 r8 15.8
- Best multiplier reached: p10 x5, p50 x15, p90 x60, p99 x252, max x630.
- insight: The curve is FINE. 23.5% is in band and deaths are spread across
  rounds 4-8. The game is not too easy and not too hard on SCHOLAR. So the
  "not fun" complaint is NOT a balance problem, and rebalancing numbers would
  have been the wrong fix. It is a decision-variety problem.

## technique — Kill the Crown Jewel

- idea: Kill LENSES entirely for a round. What carries the hand? Only figures
  and tags. Proves the base game is thin without the shop - and points at
  figures as the thing to thicken.
- idea: Kill the TARGET. If a round had no number and instead asked for a
  specific shape, the game becomes a puzzle. Ordeal candidate rather than a
  whole redesign.
- idea: Kill the HAND REFILL. Covered above, and it survives this test: a round
  as one managed hand is still recognisably Overtone.
- idea: Kill the MULTIPLIER. A game of pure addition would collapse - so the
  multiplier is load-bearing and must not be touched.
- insight: The only crown jewel that can survive being killed is the flat
  figure payout. Nothing in the game depends on a COLUMN paying 150 forever.
  That is the safest place to put an escalating decision.

## technique — Crank the Dial to 11

- idea: Crank FIGURES. Not +10% per repeat - make the fifth MONOGRAM pay double
  the first. Big enough that the player reorganises their whole run around it.
- idea: Crank ORDEALS. Fire one every round instead of three per run. 8 of 9
  Ordeals per run, a different rule every round.
- idea: Crank ORDEAL VISIBILITY. Show all remaining Ordeals at the shop so the
  player buys against a known future.
- idea: Crank ORDEALS down instead - one Ordeal, but it lasts the whole second
  half of the run.
- idea: Crank HAND SIZE up over the run: 7, 8, 9. More combinations later, so
  late rounds are richer rather than merely bigger.
- idea: Crank the SHOP. Three Lenses offered becomes one Lens, one word-burn,
  one figure-level. Three different KINDS of upgrade, not three of the same.
- insight: Cranking figures and cranking ordeal frequency are the two cranks
  that add a decision rather than a number. Everything else adds a number.

## technique — Job to Be Done

- insight: What is a player hiring ONE HAND of Overtone to do? Not "score
  points". They are hiring it to find a shape nobody handed them. The pleasure
  is recognition, not arithmetic.
- insight: What is a player hiring a RUN to do? To get better at seeing shapes.
  The run currently records money and multiplier - it records nothing about
  seeing.
- idea: If the job is recognition, the game must PAY for recognition and pay
  MORE as recognition gets sharper. Figure levelling is the literal mechanical
  statement of the job to be done.
- idea: Name the levels so the ledger reads as mastery: a COLUMN at level 3 is
  not "COLUMN x3", it is the player's COLUMN, sharpened.
- idea: Show the ledger during selection, not only in a menu, so the player
  sees which shape is worth most to THEM while choosing.
- idea: The Demand already names tags it wants. It could also name the figure
  the round rewards, so recognition has a target.
- idea: A first-time figure in a run pays a discovery bonus, so the job "learn
  to see all five" has its own reward line.
- insight: The 49.3% APPRENTICE win rate is not the problem to fix directly.
  A player who wins half their runs and feels nothing is worse off than one
  who loses and feels they got sharper. Fix the mastery signal, then re-measure.

## technique — Worst Possible Idea (then flip)

- idea (bad): Make every round randomly change every rule with no warning.
  FLIP: change one rule per round, announced one round ahead. That is Ordeals
  every round with the Bookseller naming the next - already half built.
- idea (bad): Make the player grind the same figure 50 times for a tiny bonus.
  FLIP: a short ladder with a hard cap - 3 or 4 levels, big steps, visible end.
- idea (bad): Let the player level every figure at once for free.
  FLIP: level only what you PLAY, so the ledger is a record of choices, and
  breadth costs depth.
- idea (bad): Hide the ledger so the player cannot plan around it.
  FLIP: put it on the board permanently, on the figure line that already exists.
- idea (bad): Make levelling reset every round.
  FLIP: it persists for the run and resets between runs - which is exactly what
  makes a roguelite run feel like a story.
- idea (bad): Level the figure's MULTIPLIER without limit.
  FLIP: level the CHIPS generously and the multiplier stingily, because mult is
  the runaway term the previous session already priced carefully.

## technique — Persona Journey (three players, one run each)

- FIRST RUN player, round 1: clears with one play, watches 3 more plays they did
  not need. insight: rounds 1-2 kill 0-1.8% of runs. The opening is a cutscene.
- idea: Let a big overshoot end the round early and bank the surplus. The reward
  for playing well becomes TIME, which is the thing the complaint is about.
- FIRST RUN player, round 4: meets THE DROUGHT, loses a tool they just learned.
  insight: their first structural variety is a subtraction. Their first
  impression of "the game changes" is "the game takes things away".
- idea: Give the first structural change to the player as an ADDITION - the
  first figure level - so "the game changes" reads as growth before it reads
  as loss.
- TENTH RUN player, round 3: already knows the shop, already knows the tags.
  insight: nothing in the game is different on run 10 than on run 1 except their
  own knowledge, which the game never acknowledges.
- idea: The ledger acknowledges it inside a run. Between runs, the carried Lens
  already does a little of this and is the one feature players notice.
- FIFTIETH RUN player: hunting a high score. insight: no reason to play a
  COLUMN over a MONOGRAM beyond raw payout, so optimal play converges to one
  line and the game is solved.
- idea: Levelling makes the optimal line DIVERGE - committing to a shape early
  is a strategy, and different runs commit differently. Replay value from one
  mechanic.
- insight: All three personas are helped by the same change, for three different
  reasons. That is the signature of a load-bearing fix rather than a patch.

## converging — Affinity Clustering, then Impact/Effort on the clusters

Five clusters came out of the 85:

  A. MASTERY LEDGER — figure levelling, the discovery bonus, naming levels,
     showing the ledger on the board, breadth-vs-depth.
  B. FINITE RESOURCES — hand does not refill mid-round, words tire, deck
     depletes, discards bank a charge.
  C. ROUTE AND CHOICE — choose the blind, branch the round, skip for a reward,
     buy your own Ordeal, sell a Lens back, burn a word.
  D. ORDEAL DENSITY — fire more of the 9, show them earlier, one per round.
  E. OVERSHOOT MATTERS — bank the surplus, end the round early, greed raises
     the next target.

- decision: Cluster A is the pick. Highest impact (it is the one change all
  three personas need, for three different reasons), lowest effort (FIG_PAY is
  a flat lookup table; levelling is a multiplier on a lookup plus a per-run
  counter), and it is the only cluster that escalates the DECISION rather than
  a number.
- decision: Cluster D is the cheap second. 9 Ordeals exist and 3 fire. Raising
  APPRENTICE from 1 to 2 and SCHOLAR from 3 to 4 spends content already written
  and tested. Deferred to a follow-up, not this change.
- decision: Cluster E is the cheap third and pairs naturally with A. Deferred.
- decision: Clusters B and C are REJECTED for this pass. Both are large
  rewrites of round structure with real risk to a 336-test suite and a curve
  that currently measures healthy at 23.5%. Revisit only if A does not move the
  complaint.
- decision: Rebalancing the target curve is explicitly REJECTED. The simulator
  says 23.5% and deaths spread across rounds 4-8. The numbers are not the
  problem; touching them would be motion without progress.

## synthesis — the connections across techniques

- insight: Three techniques arrived at the same place from different directions
  and did not know it. Causal Loop Mapping found that the run has ONE feedback
  loop and nothing the player learns feeds back into it. Job to Be Done found
  that the thing a player is really hiring a hand for is RECOGNITION of a shape.
  Trait Transfer found that every comparable game escalates the decision rather
  than the number, and that Balatro's hand levels are the most-cited reason its
  loop has a spine. Those are one idea wearing three hats: the game must pay
  for recognition, and pay MORE as recognition sharpens.
- insight: The Persona Journey result is the quiet one that matters most. The
  same change helps the first-time player (their first structural change becomes
  an addition instead of a subtraction), the tenth-run player (the game finally
  acknowledges what they have learned) and the fiftieth-run player (the optimal
  line diverges, so runs stop converging on one solved strategy). A change that
  helps three personas for three different reasons is load-bearing, not a patch.
- insight: Kill the Crown Jewel quietly de-risked the whole thing. Lenses, the
  target and the multiplier are all load-bearing and must not be touched. The
  flat figure payout is the ONLY crown jewel that can be killed without anything
  else collapsing - nothing in the file depends on a COLUMN paying 150 forever.
- insight: The single most useful number of the session is the one that says do
  NOT do the obvious thing. 23.5% win rate, deaths spread over rounds 4-8: the
  curve is healthy. "Not fun" was never a balance complaint, and a session that
  started by retuning TARGETS would have shipped motion instead of progress.
- insight: Worst Possible Idea produced the guardrail, by flipping. Level the
  CHIPS generously and the MULTIPLIER stingily, cap the ladder short and
  visible, level only what you actually play, and persist for the run only.
  Every one of those is a flipped bad idea and together they are the design.

- decision: CHOSEN DIRECTION - the figure ledger. Every figure you complete is
  remembered for the run; the next one of that shape pays more; the ledger is
  visible on the board while choosing; a short capped ladder; chips scale
  generously, multiplier scales slowly; resets between runs.
- decision: Deferred to follow-ups, in order - Ordeal density (cluster D),
  overshoot banking (cluster E). Rejected for this pass - finite resources
  (B) and route/choice (C), both round-structure rewrites.
