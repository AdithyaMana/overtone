01

Causal Loop Mapping

The run has exactly one feedback loop.

Insight It is a pure positive loop with no second input, so once it is running the player is a spectator, and when it is not they are dead.
Insight Nothing the player learns feeds back into the run. Skill has no accumulator. A player who has spotted four CHAINs is in the identical mechanical position to one who has spotted none.
  • Add a second accumulator the player drives with play rather than money — a figure ledger. Every figure you complete is remembered for the run, and the next one of that shape pays more.
  • Make the ledger visible as a row of five figure marks that fill in. A progress bar the player writes with their own hands.
  • Give the ledger a cost as well as a gain so it is a decision: repeating a figure levels it, but a NEW figure pays a one-off bonus. Breadth vs depth, every single hand.

Other second-loop candidates

DiscardsThe discard pile feeds something. Discards are currently a pure sink, information destroyed.
Deck thinningWords you have played become unavailable, so the deck thins toward your worst cards and forces new shapes late.
Reactive demandThe round's Demand reacts to what you played last hand, so repeating yourself pays less.
Insight A roguelite loop needs a threat that also escalates from player action. Overtone's only threat is the target, and the target is on rails.
  • The Bookseller could raise the next target if you overshoot this one — greed is punished, so overshooting becomes a decision instead of a freebie.
  • Overshoot could instead bank into money, making the size of the clear matter.

Clearing by 40,000 and clearing by 1 being identical is the single largest dead signal in the game. Every hand after the target is met is ceremony.

02

Assumption Reversal

Twelve rules of the loop, each held up and turned over.

the hand refills to 7 every play
⇄
The hand only refills at the end of a round. A round is one hand of 7+ managed across 4 plays, and every card spent is a card gone. Instant escalation inside a round.
you play up to 3
⇄
Round 1 lets you play 1, and the cap rises as the run goes — 1, 2, 3, then 4. The verb itself grows.
more cards is better
⇄
A rising cap means early rounds are tight and readable and late rounds are combinatorially rich. The learning curve and the difficulty curve finally point the same way.
the target is fixed when the round starts
⇄
A target that climbs while you play — every hand raises the bar for the next — turns four plays into a race rather than four withdrawals from the same bank.
figures are found
⇄
The round names a figure it wants and pays double for it. Now you hunt a specific shape rather than accepting whatever falls out.
ordeals are scheduled
⇄
The player chooses their Ordeal at the shop in exchange for money or a Lens. Voluntary difficulty is the oldest roguelite engine there is.
the Bookseller sells
⇄
The Bookseller buys. Sell him a Lens you outgrew for money, so the five slots become a live decision instead of a filling queue.
you keep your deck
⇄
A round takes three random words out of your deck permanently, making the deck a resource that depletes.
all 8 rounds are played
⇄
A big overshoot skips the next round and takes its reward — a risk/greed lever with real teeth.
words are neutral
⇄
Words the player has already played this run get tired and pay less, forcing rotation through the deck.
the player picks order
⇄
A figure that requires a specific order the round dictates — descending only, this round — is an Ordeal that changes the decision rather than removing a tool.
discards are free information loss
⇄
Discarding banks a small charge that buys a re-deal or a peek later.

The strongest reversals all do the same thing: they make a resource that is currently infinite — cards, order freedom, target stability — finite. Stagnancy is what an infinite resource feels like.

03

Trait Transfer

Graft from games that solved this exact problem.

The donor worth naming precisely Balatro has the identical skeleton — pick 5 of 8, 3 hands per blind, jokers multiply. It does not feel stagnant. The difference is worth naming precisely rather than admiring.
Balatro · trait 1 Hand levels Every poker hand type has a level; playing a Flush levels Flush, permanently, for the run. GraftThis is exactly figure levelling, and it is the single most-cited reason Balatro's loop has a spine.
Balatro · trait 2 The blind is chosen Small / Big / Boss — and you may skip Small or Big for a tag reward. GraftChoice before the round, not just after it.
Balatro · trait 3 The Boss is revealed first The Boss Blind is shown before you shop, so the shop is a decision about the specific wall ahead. GraftOvertone names its Ordeal already; it does not let you buy against it.
Slay the Spire The map You choose your route and therefore your risk. GraftOvertone's 8 rounds are a corridor. After each round, two doors: a harder target for a better Lens, or an easier one for money. Eight binary choices is a run you authored.
Into the Breach Perfect information A short puzzle with no randomness to hide behind. GraftOvertone could show the next hand's top card, making a play a plan rather than a reaction.
Luck be a Landlord The deck shrinks as a choice Removal is a reward. GraftOvertone has no removal at all, so the deck only ever gets noisier. The Bookseller offers to burn a word — in a game about finding shapes, cutting your worst word is the most legible upgrade there is.
Dicey Dungeons Same tools, different rules Every episode radically re-rules the same kit. GraftOvertone's 9 Ordeals are exactly this, and it fires 3 of them.
Slay the Spire The card that changes how you play Not how much you score. Overtone's Lenses are almost all multipliers; none of them changes the verb. Verb-changing Lens candidates play 4 wordssee 9 cardsreorder after seeing the scoreplay the same word twicelock one card across hands

Every game named here escalates the decision, not just the number. Balatro levels the hand, Spire branches the route, Dicey changes the rule. Overtone escalates only the number. That is the whole diagnosis in one line.

Crank the Dial to 11

Not +10%. Far enough that the player reorganises around it.

Figures 1 → 11
Not +10% per repeat — make the fifth MONOGRAM pay double the first. Big enough that the player reorganises their whole run around it. adds a decision
Ordeals 1 → 11
Fire one every round instead of three per run. 8 of 9 Ordeals per run, a different rule every round. adds a decision
Ordeal visibility 1 → 11
Show all remaining Ordeals at the shop so the player buys against a known future.
Ordeals, cranked down 11 → 1
One Ordeal — but it lasts the whole second half of the run.
Hand size 7 → 8 → 9
Grow it over the run. More combinations later, so late rounds are richer rather than merely bigger.
The shop 1 → 11
Three Lenses offered becomes one Lens, one word-burn, one figure-level. Three different kinds of upgrade, not three of the same.

Cranking figures and cranking Ordeal frequency are the two cranks that add a decision. Everything else adds a number.

06

Job to Be Done

The pleasure is recognition, not arithmetic.

Hired for — one hand
not: score points
Find a shape nobody handed them.
The pleasure is recognition, not arithmetic.
Hired for — one run
the run records: money, multiplier
Get better at seeing shapes.
It records nothing about seeing.
  • If the job is recognition, the game must pay for recognition and pay MORE as recognition gets sharper. Figure levelling is the literal mechanical statement of the job to be done.
  • Name the levels so the ledger reads as mastery: a COLUMN at level 3 is not “COLUMN ×3”, it is the player's COLUMN, sharpened.
  • Show the ledger during selection, not only in a menu, so the player sees which shape is worth most to THEM while choosing.
  • The Demand already names tags it wants. It could also name the figure the round rewards, so recognition has a target.
  • A first-time figure in a run pays a discovery bonus, so the job “learn to see all five” has its own reward line.
Insight The 49.3% APPRENTICE win rate is not the problem to fix directly. A player who wins half their runs and feels nothing is worse off than one who loses and feels they got sharper. Fix the mastery signal, then re-measure.

Worst Possible Idea

Then flip. Every guardrail in the final design came from here.

WorstMake every round randomly change every rule with no warning.
↻
FlipChange one rule per round, announced one round ahead. That is Ordeals every round with the Bookseller naming the next — already half built.
WorstMake the player grind the same figure 50 times for a tiny bonus.
↻
FlipA short ladder with a hard cap — 3 or 4 levels, big steps, visible end.
WorstLet the player level every figure at once for free.
↻
FlipLevel only what you play, so the ledger is a record of choices, and breadth costs depth.
WorstHide the ledger so the player cannot plan around it.
↻
FlipPut it on the board permanently, on the figure line that already exists.
WorstMake levelling reset every round.
↻
FlipIt persists for the run and resets between runs — which is exactly what makes a roguelite run feel like a story.
WorstLevel the figure's multiplier without limit.
↻

Persona Journey

Three players, one run each.

First run
Learning what the game is.
Round 1
Clears with one play, then watches 3 more plays they did not need.
Rounds 1–2 kill 0–1.8% of runs. The opening is a cutscene.
Let a big overshoot end the round early and bank the surplus. The reward for playing well becomes time — which is the thing the complaint is about.
Round 4
Meets THE DROUGHT, and loses a tool they had just learned.
Their first structural variety is a subtraction. Their first impression of “the game changes” is “the game takes things away”.
Give the first structural change as an addition — the first figure level — so “the game changes” reads as growth before it reads as loss.
Tenth run
Knows the furniture.
Round 3
Already knows the shop. Already knows the tags.
Nothing in the game is different on run 10 than on run 1 except their own knowledge — which the game never acknowledges.
The ledger acknowledges it inside a run. Between runs, the carried Lens already does a little of this, and it is the one feature players notice.
Fiftieth run
Hunting a high score.
Whole run
Plays for the number, not the surprise.
There is no reason to play a COLUMN over a MONOGRAM beyond raw payout, so optimal play converges to one line and the game is solved.
Levelling makes the optimal line diverge. Committing to a shape early is a strategy, and different runs commit differently. Replay value from one mechanic.

All three personas are helped by the same change, for three different reasons. That is the signature of a load-bearing fix rather than a patch.

Synthesis

One idea wearing three hats.

The connections across techniques — what the session actually found once the eight lines of attack were laid on top of one another.

Convergence 01

Three techniques arrived at the same place and did not know it.

Causal Loop Mapping found that the run has one feedback loop and nothing the player learns feeds back into it. Job to Be Done found that the thing a player is really hiring a hand for is recognition of a shape. Trait Transfer found that every comparable game escalates the decision rather than the number, and that Balatro's hand levels are the most-cited reason its loop has a spine.

Those are one idea wearing three hats: the game must pay for recognition, and pay more as recognition sharpens.

Convergence 02

The quiet result that matters most.

The same change helps the first-time player (their first structural change becomes an addition instead of a subtraction), the tenth-run player (the game finally acknowledges what they have learned) and the fiftieth-run player (the optimal line diverges, so runs stop converging on one solved strategy).

A change that helps three personas for three different reasons is load-bearing, not a patch.

Convergence 05 — the guardrails

Worst Possible Idea produced the design, by flipping.

Every constraint below is a flipped bad idea. Together they are the design.

  • Level the CHIPS generously
  • Level the MULTIPLIER stingily
  • Cap the ladder short and visible
  • Level only what you actually play
  • Persist for the run only
  • Keep it on the board, never in a menu
Chosen direction

The figure ledger

Every figure you complete is remembered for the run. The next one of that shape pays more. The ledger is visible on the board while choosing. A short capped ladder: chips scale generously, multiplier scales slowly. It resets between runs.
