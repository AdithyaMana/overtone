## Measured ground truth (before any idea)

- balance.js 400 runs, optimal play: win rate 22.3% (healthy). Deaths by round:
  r1 0.0% · r2 0.0% · r3 2.5% · r4 12.3% · r5 15.3% · r6 20.8% · r7 8.5% · r8 40.8%
- balance.js --curve: shipped targets sit at percentiles p0 p0 p8 p15 p16 p31 p25 p53
  of what a round can actually produce.
- TARGETS = [350, 800, 5200, 9200, 19000, 23000, 34000, 46000]
  ratios 2.29 / 6.50 / 1.77 / 2.07 / 1.21 / 1.48 / 1.35
- One hand of animation: events*gap (210 or 130) + 420 if a figure + 240 + 1250.
  Typical 3.2s-4.0s, all of it with G.animating locking every input.

## technique — Failure Analysis

- insight: Rounds 1 and 2 kill 0.0% of runs across 400 runs. The first 3 rounds of every run are a formality with no stakes.
- insight: Difficulty is a step function, not a ramp. 40.8% of all runs die on round 8 alone; round 7 kills less than round 6.
- insight: The 800 -> 5200 jump at round 3 is 6.5x. Everything before it is free and everything after is a cliff. Learning and playing never overlap.
- insight: Round 5 -> 6 is 1.21x. A round that asks almost nothing more than the last is a round with no news in it. Literal stagnation in the curve.
- idea: The early-round problem is ceremony-to-decision ratio, not lethality. Round 1 target 350 against a p3 ceiling of 1784 means one play clears it, then the player watches 3.5s of scoring, 2.1s of celebration and reads a 400-word shop.
- idea: terminal sleep(1250) after the total lands is pure dead air. Nothing animates, nothing accepts input.
- idea: gap has two values only (210 / 130). A 5-event hand and a 20-event hand feel identical per beat, so long hands run 3x longer instead of denser.
- idea: G.animating locks selection as well as play. The player cannot even think ahead with their hands.
- idea: the figure event holds gap+420 at the very START of every hand, before anything scores. The slowest beat is the first one, every single time.
- idea: skipAnim exists (space, or click the stage) and is never taught anywhere in the game.
- idea: round transition stacks celebrate 980ms + stamp 1150ms + modal shop. 5-10 seconds out of the game, every round, 8 times.
- idea: discards give no feedback worth the tap - no sense of having improved the hand.
- idea: nothing escalates WITHIN a round. Play 1 and play 4 are mechanically identical; the only tension is the number.

## technique — Crank the Dial to 11

- idea: budget a fixed TOTAL animation time per hand and divide by event count, so a hand always takes the same wall-clock time and long hands get denser instead of longer. Gives the game a rhythm.
- idea: cut per-event floor to ~55-90ms and let chip-flies overlap instead of strictly queueing.
- idea: kill the terminal hold; let the total fade while the player is already selecting the next hand.
- idea: un-gate selection from G.animating. Only gate play() itself. Queue the next decision.
- idea: an explicit speed control, persisted, defaulting to fast.
- idea: after N hands the game stops showing the full performance by itself - it learns you do not need the lesson.
- idea: pitch the score SFX up per event index so a 15-event hand is an audible rising run that resolves.
- idea: scale the multiplier readout physically with its value - an x18 hand should LOOK different from an x2 before you read the digits.
- idea: continuous shake intensity from weight rather than 4 buckets.
- idea: overlap the shop's entrance with the confetti rather than queueing it.
- idea: reserve the full slow performance for the hand that actually clears the round. Every other hand is quick.

## technique — Kill the Crown Jewel (the prose is the jewel)

- idea: the shop explains itself in ~400 words instead of showing itself. Replace with one line and three readable cards.
- idea: the reality check runs up to 7 full deck passes to produce a paragraph. Make it a bar: your ceiling against the target.
- idea: Ordeal and Reckoning warnings are two more prose boxes. One icon and six words each.
- idea: make the shop non-modal - a strip over the board, so the player never leaves the table.

## technique — Trait Transfer

- idea: Balatro - chips and mult tick WHILE cards are still flipping. Overlapping beats, not sequential ones.
- idea: Balatro - blind selection. You choose which round to take and may skip one for a reward. Agency between rounds with no prose.
- idea: Vampire Survivors - the reward screen is three big icons and one click.
- idea: Tetris - the drop is instant; the payoff is the clear, not the travel. Overtone spends its whole budget on the travel.
- idea: Slay the Spire - a visible map gives every round a position in a story. Overtone's rounds are a flat list of numbers.
- idea: Peggle - slow-mo is for the FINAL shot. Overtone animates every hand like a final shot.
- idea: Threes - input is never blocked, moves queue.
- idea: Luck be a Landlord - the board resolves in under a second and the fun is reading the aftermath, not watching the process.

## technique — Ship in 60 Minutes (leverage per minute)

- idea: two constants (gap 210->90, hold 1250->400) roughly halve hand time. Minutes of work.
- idea: time-budget gap function - the single highest-value change in the file.
- idea: un-gate selection during resolution.
- idea: re-derive TARGETS as a monotone ramp and validate with balance.js --targets, which scores a candidate curve without editing the game.
- idea: default the animation to fast once the tutorial is done.

## decisions

- decision: Do NOT change the overall win rate. 22.3% is healthy. Redistribute WHERE the danger is, keeping the total.
- decision: Round 1 stays clearable by a naive player (floor median 627). The fix for early rounds is engagement density, not lethality.
- decision: Ship three things - (A) animation/pacing rebuild, (B) monotone curve validated by simulation, (C) cut the ceremony and prose between rounds.

## technique — converge

- decision: Ship A (pacing), B (curve), C (shop prose). Everything else on the list stays on the list.
- insight: measured in the browser against the real script, a hand went 3260/3238/3022ms
  (bare / 3 Lenses / 5 Lenses) to 1214/1217/1032ms. The second number matters as much as the
  first: hand length is now flat across deck size, which it never was.
- insight: the 38ms beat floor was too aggressive. Every event makes a label, a flying chip and
  a counter roll, so the beat sets DOM work per second, and at 38ms it starved the main thread —
  the same complaint arriving as dropped frames instead of as waiting. Floor is 110ms.
- insight: the figure label was the one compression that cost more than it bought. It carries the
  longest string the animation prints and it names a thing rather than printing a number, so it
  keeps a 620ms beat of its own against the old 630ms. Everything else got faster; that did not.
- insight: cutting the figure label's life to "clear the lane" was a mistake — it has its own lane
  and z-index, so the overlap was never real, and shortening it opened a window where the figure
  had gone and the cards had not started and the stage was empty.
- decision: two of the shop copy edits were reverted. "ORDEAL NEXT" reads like a stub where
  "NEXT ROUND IS AN ORDEAL" reads like a sentence, and the slot line lost the word carrying its
  meaning. Shorter is not the goal; shorter AND clear is.
- insight: round 8 kills 40-48% of runs under every curve tried, because rounds 4, 6 and 8 are
  Ordeals — output DROPS there while the target peaks. So the fix is not a smaller round 8, it is
  accepting that Ordeal rounds take their difficulty from their rule and letting their numbers
  climb least. The Bookseller already names the Ordeal before you spend.

## correction — a column header I misread

- insight: balance.js prints "How far a run gets", and the round 8 row counts the runs that WON as
  well as the ones that died there. Read as a death count it says round 8 ends 40.8% of runs. It
  ends 18.5%. Two candidate curves were spent chasing a wall that was an artefact of the misreading.
- insight: with the rows read correctly the real old shape is 0/0/2.5/12.3/15.3/20.8/8.5/18.5. The
  front is still empty, which was the main finding and survives. But the second finding changes:
  the problem in the back half is not round 8, it is round 7 killing less than half what round 6
  does — a round with no news in it between two that have some.
- decision: ship 500, 2900, 5600, 9600, 20000, 22000, 36000, 40000. Measured
  0/1.3/3.3/12.0/11.3/18.7/12.0/16.0 at a 25.3% win rate, against 22.3% before. Rounds 2, 3 and 7
  now carry a share they did not.
- insight: APPRENTICE is a per-round FRACTION of SCHOLAR, so moving SCHOLAR moved it silently —
  its round 2 would have gone from 480 to 1,740 against a beginner floor of 842. Every fraction
  re-derived to hold it where it was already tested, except rounds 1 and 2, raised on purpose.
- insight: the beat floor was raised from 60ms to 110ms on a guess about main-thread starvation.
  Recording frame intervals says the guess was wrong — median 16.7ms, nothing over 50ms, on both a
  bare deck and a five-Lens one. The flake it was meant to fix was two other bugs: a stale file
  snapshot that silently reverted two edits, and the figure label dying before its own beat ended.
  Floor is 70ms, which is where labels stop being readable rather than where frames suffer.

## correction — round 1 is not round 2

- insight: raising round 1 from 350 to 500 broke three tests, all of them tests that clear round 1
  by playing the first three cards as dealt — which is exactly the beginner the floor measures.
- insight: the argument "a round nobody can lose is a round with nothing in it" is right about
  round 2 and wrong about round 1, and the difference is that round 1 is the tutorial. 500 sits
  near the 30th percentile of the beginner floor: it adds no decision, it just fails one beginner
  in three on the first screen they ever see. The file already said this and I overrode it.
- decision: round 1 back to 350. Round 2 is the one that moves, 800 to 2,900.
- insight: a full suite run was started and then index.html was edited while it was running, so
  six failures were half real and half torn reads. Re-run clean before diagnosing anything.
- insight: with round 1 at 350 the curve measures 0/1.0/4.5/12.0/9.5/17.0/12.0 and a 16.5% loss at
  round 8, win 27.5%. Shape is right — round 2 and round 7 now carry a share, and round 7 is no
  longer the dip between two harder rounds — but 27.5% against 22.3% is a game that got easier,
  which nobody asked for. Rounds 5 and 6 are the two that went soft, so they go back up.
