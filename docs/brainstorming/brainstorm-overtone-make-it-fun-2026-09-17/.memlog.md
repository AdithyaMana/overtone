## Measured ground truth (before any idea)

- Lexicon 249 words. 19 overtones. 3.23 tags per word on average, min 1, max 5.
  Word length 2 to 11 letters.
- Tag frequency is lopsided: DANGER 85 of 249, MOTION 83, NATURE 62, TIME 57 at
  one end; PLANT 20, FOOD 21, TECH 22, MONEY 22 at the other.
- 18 Demands, 30 Lenses (5 slots), 9 Ordeals.
- 8 rounds, hand of 7, play up to 3, 4 plays per round = 32 plays per run.
- 26 of 30 Lenses read MEANING (tags). All 5 figures - the multiplier source -
  read SPELLING: same first letter, letter-chaining, same length, length steps,
  same length again.
- The tutorial and the help panel both state "what a word means never scores a
  point", against a tagline of "Meaning is the physics".
- The board computes and displays the score of a selection BEFORE the player
  commits.

## The turn, written out

Scan seven tag rows. Find which cards carry a gold (demanded) tag. Check
lengths and initials for a figure. Order three. Press PLAY. Watch ~900ms of
numbers. Repeat 31 more times.

## technique: started Alien Anthropologist

- idea: The human is given seven words of their own language and the first thing they do is stop reading them.
- idea: They read small grey labels under each word. The labels say what the word means. The human already knew what the word means.
- idea: The machine prints the answer on the card before the human chooses, then congratulates them for choosing.
- idea: Three words are laid side by side and nothing happens between them. Putting two words together is the entire art, and here it is a row.
- idea: The human has a language of enormous subtlety and is counting letters with it.
- idea: CAVERN beside FROST is reported as 150. Not cold, not depth, not the specific silence of a cold hollow. 150.
- idea: Nothing on the table remembers anything. Each turn the table is as it was.
- idea: There is no opponent. The human plays against a number decided before they sat down.
- idea: Four plays, and all four are used every time. That is not a choice, it is a length.
- idea: Discarding gets different LABELS, not different words.
- idea: When the human wins, nothing exists that did not exist before. No sentence was written. No object was made.
- idea: The most beautiful thing on the table - a hand of English nouns - is the part the rules tell the human to ignore.
- idea: Nineteen categories for all of existence, and a third of everything is DANGER.
- insight: They call the shapes "figures" and the shapes are about spelling. In rhetoric a figure IS a turn of meaning - metaphor, antithesis, chiasmus. The game made a pun and does not know it.
- idea: A game about words in which the human never utters one.
- idea: Tags are drawn as small boxes. Boxes are how you store what you are not using.
- idea: The gold stripe tells the human which cards are correct, then asks them to choose.
- idea: Two humans playing on the same day produce identical play and can tell each other nothing about it afterwards.
- idea: The scoring animation is the game's most expensive moment and the human presses space to get past it.
- idea: There is a shop, and money, and a shopkeeper with a name. None of them are ever seen. The Bookseller is a menu.

## technique: started Kill the Crown Jewel (delete the Lens engine and points x multiplier)

- idea: What survives is 249 words with 19 dimensions of meaning - a semantic space. The game becomes navigation, not accountancy.
- idea: Without a multiplier there is no exponential target, so "survive 8 rounds" dies too. A run becomes a journey, a construction, or a conversation.
- idea: Chain game - play a word sharing an overtone with the last. FURNACE -(HEAT)- EMBER -(BRIGHT)- CHOIR -(LOUD)- DRONE. The chain is the artifact and it reads back as a poem.
- idea: Score becomes how far you got, how strange the path, whether you returned home.
- idea: The Demand becomes a DESTINATION rather than a filter. "Get from WINTER to FURNACE in five words."
- idea: Progression becomes the words you keep. You build a vocabulary, not a machine.
- idea: Kill the numbers and the feedback is the line you made, read aloud. The audio assets already exist.
- idea: Kill the hand. Give the whole lexicon and a constraint.
- insight: Escalate the CONSTRAINT, not the target. Round 1: say something COLD. Round 8: say something COLD and BRIGHT, without NATURE, in four words, each shorter than the last. Difficulty becomes the sentence you are asked to write.
- idea: The daily becomes: same seven words for everyone, and you compare what you SAID.
- idea: The shareable is a line of poetry rather than a number.
- idea: "Pick 3" becomes "play 1, and the table answers".
- idea: The game plays back - you play FROST, it answers with a word sharing an overtone, you must answer that. An opponent, and a rally.
- idea: Each word sounds a tone derived from its tags. A chain is a melody.
- idea: A run ends with the poem you wrote and the machine's reply to it.

## technique: started One Feature Only

- idea: Keep only this: two words next to each other make a third thing. Everything else is scaffolding.
- idea: Keep only the ontology, expressed as a space you move through rather than a table you look up.
- idea: Keep only the daily shared hand and the comparison.
- idea: Keep only the tension of spend-now or hold.
- idea: Keep only naming - the player names what they made and the game says what it means.
- insight: If one verb survives it must be JOIN, not PICK. Pick is selection. Join is creation. The whole boredom diagnosis is that Overtone's verb is PICK.
- insight: Keep only this: THE TAGS ARE HIDDEN. Then the player uses their own knowledge of English, and "meaning is the physics" is finally true. The game is boring because it prints its own answer.
- idea: Softer version - tags revealed only for words you have played before, so the lexicon is something you learn across runs.
- idea: The round's Demand is a WORD, not a tag list. "THE THAW wants words like THAW." You judge the likeness yourself.
- idea: Reveal a card's tags on demand, but it costs something.

## technique: started Shadow Work Mining

- idea: The thing being avoided - Overtone is Balatro with a thesaurus. Everyone can feel the skeleton. The words are a costume on a scoring machine.
- idea: 249 words is small. A word game people replay needs either a large vocabulary or deep interaction with a small one. It currently has neither.
- idea: The game is solvable by brute force. The preview scores the selection, so an optimiser scans 210 ordered triples. The "decision" is arithmetic the game already did.
- idea: Nothing is at stake inside a turn. You cannot lose a turn. You can only score less.
- idea: There is no interesting failure. You simply do not reach a number.
- idea: The theme is gorgeous and the verbs are accountancy.
- insight: Every recent improvement - the scoring animation, the figure ledger, the balance passes - polished the scoring layer. The scoring layer is not the problem. The problem is one layer up: what the player is deciding.

## technique: started Dream Fusion Laboratory

- idea: Fantasy - you type a sentence, the game understands it, scores its beauty and answers in kind.
- idea: Bridge - arbitrary English cannot be parsed offline, but 249 x 19 is a tiny matrix. Precompute every PAIR's relationship: shared overtones, opposed overtones, distance.
- insight: Make word-to-word the physics, not word-to-checklist. Resonance = a shared overtone. Tension = an opposed pair (HEAT/COLD, DARK/BRIGHT, ABSTRACT/concrete). ANTONYM ENGINE is already the most interesting Lens in the game. It should be the core, not an item.
- idea: Resonance is safe and small. Tension is large and risky. Every placement becomes a judgement about meaning.
- idea: A pair with a shared overtone distils into a new card carrying only that overtone.
- idea: A pair with opposed overtones makes a paradox card - worth more, unstable.
- idea: The game reads your play back with connectives: "FROST, and then the CHOIR."
- idea: Compose left to right where each word modifies the next, adjective-to-noun.
- insight: A line you played becomes a Lens for the rest of the run, named after itself. You WRITE your jokers instead of buying them. This solves expression, persistence and the invisible shop in one move.

## technique: started Verb Hunt (what the hands actually do)

- idea: Drag one word onto another to fuse them.
- idea: Slot words into a sentence frame - "The ___ ___s the ___."
- idea: Place words on a grid where adjacency scores, a crossword of meaning.
- idea: Rotate a card to choose which of its overtones is active. A four-tag word becomes a four-sided die you orient.
- idea: Stack words so later ones inherit or overwrite tags.
- idea: Speaking a word spends it permanently. The perfect word costs you the word.
- idea: Trade - give the game a word, it gives you one back.
- idea: Bury a word to make its overtone the theme of the round.
- idea: Split a word - take one overtone off it and keep it as a token.
- idea: Sharpen a word - remove overtones so it means fewer things, more strongly.
- idea: Two words held together and pulled apart, like tuning.

## technique: started Opposition Design (why you want the next turn)

- idea: A rival who plays after you and takes overtones off the table.
- idea: A listener you must convince, who is bored by repetition - COLD twice pays less than COLD once.
- insight: FATIGUE. An overtone you used goes dull for a few turns. One rule, and the same play twice stops working - which is the whole repetition complaint, answered cheaply.
- idea: The round DRIFTS - after each play the demand moves toward what you played, or away from it.
- idea: The table answers with a word and you must top it.
- idea: A diegetic clock - the candle, the thaw, the tide - rather than a play counter.
- idea: Something on the table that persists and grows: a poem, a structure, a creature.
- idea: Remove the score preview. Let the player be wrong, and let being wrong be interesting.
- idea: Fewer, heavier turns. Twelve rich decisions beat thirty-two thin ones.
- idea: One hand per round instead of four plays, so each play carries weight.
- idea: Make discarding cost something real. Four free discards is churn.

## technique: started Figures of Speech (following the pun)

- insight: Replace the five spelling shapes with five rhetorical ones, read off the tag matrix. This closes the seam between the poetic layer and the mechanical layer, and it is the single most on-theme change available.
- idea: ANTITHESIS - two words with opposed overtones. FURNACE and FROST.
- idea: SYNONYMY - two words sharing three or more overtones. Near-repetition, safe, small.
- idea: ESCALATION - three words with a rising count of shared overtones.
- idea: JUXTAPOSITION - two words sharing nothing at all. The surreal one. Pays most, hardest to use.
- idea: SYNAESTHESIA - a word that is LOUD next to one that is BRIGHT. Already a Lens; promote it.
- idea: METONYMY - a word carrying an overtone the round wants, placed beside one that does not, lending it.
- idea: CHIASMUS - four words whose overtones mirror, A-B-B-A.
- idea: The figure NAMES itself in rhetorical language, so a player learns real words for what they just did.
- idea: Figures become the thing you collect and master across runs, which is what the ledger was reaching for but on the wrong axis.

## technique: started Structure and Shareability

- idea: A run's artifact is the poem, not the score.
- idea: Daily leaderboard ranks the best LINE, not the biggest number.
- idea: The run ends on a printable card of what you wrote.
- idea: Words age - one you keep across rounds wears, and its meaning drifts.
- idea: Words combine into words not in the lexicon, and the game names them.
- idea: The deck is a library and you are the librarian. Rounds are requests from readers.
- idea: Overtone as a duet - you and the machine alternate, building one poem together.
- idea: Keep the daily seed. It is the one piece of the current structure that creates conversation.

## Synthesis

- insight: THE VERB IS WRONG. Overtone's verb is PICK - choose 3 of 7 against a checklist the game has already scored for you. Every symptom the players report follows from it: no skill expression, no composition, no surprise, no artifact, no reason to tell anyone about it. The fix is not a better scoring curve. It is a different verb: JOIN.
- insight: The game already contains its own answer twice over. ANTONYM ENGINE - "a HEAT word straight after a COLD one" - is the only Lens where two words in the hand interact with each other, and it is the most interesting thing in the file. And "figure" already means a turn of MEANING in rhetoric while the code uses it for spelling. Both are the game pointing at the design it should have had.
- insight: Hiding the tags is the load-bearing change. While the card prints its own overtones and the board prints the score before you commit, there is no judgement left to make and no way to be cleverer than the person next to you. Hide them and the player's own sense of English becomes the skill - which is exactly what "meaning is the physics" promised.
- insight: FATIGUE is the cheapest large win. One rule - an overtone dulls after you use it - and the identical-every-turn complaint is answered without touching anything else.
- insight: Written Lenses beat bought Lenses. A line you actually played, kept as a permanent effect and named after itself, gives the run an object that is yours, makes the Bookseller visible, and turns a score into something worth showing another person.
- decision: Direction taken forward - THE VERB BECOMES JOIN. Five changes, in dependency order: (1) hide the overtones, (2) score word-to-word resonance and tension instead of word-to-checklist, (3) replace the five spelling figures with rhetorical figures read off the tag matrix, (4) overtone fatigue, (5) lines you play become the Lenses you keep. Structural companions: fewer and heavier turns, and no score preview.
- decision: Everything survives the rebuild except the scoring maths. Lexicon, ontology, card art, audio, the daily seed, the Demands and most Lens concepts all carry over.

## Measured after the session, to test the direction

The direction rests on one claim: that the existing tag matrix has enough
texture to be the physics. Measured on the real lexicon, all 30,876 pairs:

- resonant (share an overtone)      49.7%
- in tension only (opposed pair)    17.0%
- silent (nothing in common)        33.2%
- BOTH resonant and in tension      11.9%   <- the poetically interesting ones
- shared-overtone count: 0 in 50.3% of pairs, 1 in 36.9%, 2 in 11.2%, 3+ in 1.7%

Inside a real dealt hand of seven (400 opening hands, 8,400 pairs):

- resonant 49.2%, in tension 29.6%, silent 33.1%
- 99.5% of opening hands hold at least one pair in tension

The oppositions used: HEAT/COLD, DARK/BRIGHT, NATURE/TECH, MIND/BODY,
WET/HEAT, and ABSTRACT against BODY, FOOD and TOOL. All already in the
ontology; nothing new was authored to get these numbers.

- insight: The comparison that decides it. The CURRENT figure system makes
  nothing at all from three random cards 43% of the time and THE PAIR - which
  demonstrates nothing - another 44%, so it produces an interesting shape in
  about one hand in eight. The meaning matrix already in the file produces a
  tension in 99.5% of opening hands and a resonance in half of all pairs. The
  interesting game is sitting in the data the game already has and declines to
  read.
- insight: 1,469 double antitheses exist in the lexicon right now. OWL / EYE
  opposes on DARK/BRIGHT and on MIND/BODY at the same time. Overtone currently
  scores that pair as nothing.
