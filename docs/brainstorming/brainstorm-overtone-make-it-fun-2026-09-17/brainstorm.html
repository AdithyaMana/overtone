OVERTONE

A word game about the overtones of meaning, which turned out to be sitting on a better game than the one built on top of it.

Topic

Overtone plays boring. The idea is praised, the playing is not. What would make the moment-to-moment actually fun?

Goal

A direction for the core loop, with permission to rebuild it from scratch. The assets stay — 249-word lexicon, 19-tag ontology, card art, audio. The loop does not have to.

RUN I

Alien Anthropologist

“The most beautiful thing on the table — a hand of English nouns — is the part the rules tell the human to ignore.”

  1. OBS 01The human is given seven words of their own language and the first thing they do is stop reading them.
  2. OBS 02They read small grey labels under each word. The labels say what the word means. The human already knew what the word means.
  3. OBS 03The machine prints the answer on the card before the human chooses, then congratulates them for choosing.
  4. OBS 04Three words are laid side by side and nothing happens between them. Putting two words together is the entire art, and here it is a row.
  5. OBS 05The human has a language of enormous subtlety and is counting letters with it.
  6. OBS 06Nothing on the table remembers anything. Each turn the table is as it was.
  7. OBS 07There is no opponent. The human plays against a number decided before they sat down.
  8. OBS 08Four plays, and all four are used every time. That is not a choice, it is a length.
  9. OBS 09Discarding gets different LABELS, not different words.
  10. OBS 10When the human wins, nothing exists that did not exist before. No sentence was written. No object was made.
  11. OBS 11Nineteen categories for all of existence, and a third of everything is DANGER.
  12. OBS 12A game about words in which the human never utters one.
  13. OBS 13Tags are drawn as small boxes. Boxes are how you store what you are not using.
  14. OBS 14The gold stripe tells the human which cards are correct, then asks them to choose.
  15. OBS 15Two humans playing on the same day produce identical play and can tell each other nothing about it afterwards.
  16. OBS 16The scoring animation is the game's most expensive moment and the human presses space to get past it.
  17. OBS 17There is a shop, and money, and a shopkeeper with a name. None of them are ever seen. The Bookseller is a menu.
Insight — the pun the game made and does not know it

They call the shapes “figures” and the shapes are about spelling. In rhetoric a figure is a turn of meaning — metaphor, antithesis, chiasmus.

Removed

What survives
  • 249 words with 19 dimensions of meaning — a semantic space. The game becomes navigation, not accountancy.
  • Without a multiplier there is no exponential target, so “survive 8 rounds” dies too. A run becomes a journey, a construction, or a conversation.

Insight — escalate the CONSTRAINT, not the target

Difficulty becomes the sentence you are asked to write.

  1. Round 1Say something COLD.
  2. Round 8Say something COLD and BRIGHT, without NATURE, in four words, each shorter than the last.
  • Score becomes how far you got, how strange the path, whether you returned home.
  • The Demand becomes a DESTINATION rather than a filter. “Get from WINTER to FURNACE in five words.”
  • Progression becomes the words you keep. You build a vocabulary, not a machine.
  • Kill the numbers and the feedback is the line you made, read aloud. The audio assets already exist.
  • Kill the hand. Give the whole lexicon and a constraint.
  • The daily becomes: same seven words for everyone, and you compare what you SAID.
  • The shareable is a line of poetry rather than a number.
  • “Pick 3” becomes “play 1, and the table answers”.
  • The game plays back — you play FROST, it answers with a word sharing an overtone, you must answer that. An opponent, and a rally.
  • Each word sounds a tone derived from its tags. A chain is a melody.
  • A run ends with the poem you wrote and the machine's reply to it.

RUN IV

Shadow Work Mining

The thing being avoided

  1. −1Overtone is Balatro with a thesaurus. Everyone can feel the skeleton. The words are a costume on a scoring machine.
  2. −2249 words is small. A word game people replay needs either a large vocabulary or deep interaction with a small one. It currently has neither.
  3. −3The game is solvable by brute force. The preview scores the selection, so an optimiser scans 210 ordered triples. The “decision” is arithmetic the game already did.
  4. −4Nothing is at stake inside a turn. You cannot lose a turn. You can only score less.
  5. −5There is no interesting failure. You simply do not reach a number.
  6. −6The theme is gorgeous and the verbs are accountancy.
Bedrock

Every recent improvement — the scoring animation, the figure ledger, the balance passes — polished the scoring layer. The scoring layer is not the problem. The problem is one layer up: what the player is deciding.

RUN VI

Verb Hunt

What the hands actually do

DRAG

One word onto another, to fuse them.

SLOT

Words into a sentence frame — “The ___ ___s the ___.”

PLACE

Words on a grid where adjacency scores. A crossword of meaning.

ROTATE

A card, to choose which overtone is active. A four-tag word becomes a four-sided die you orient.

STACK

Words, so later ones inherit or overwrite tags.

SPEAK

A word, and spend it permanently. The perfect word costs you the word.

TRADE

Give the game a word, it gives you one back.

BURY

A word, to make its overtone the theme of the round.

SPLIT

A word — take one overtone off it and keep it as a token.

SHARPEN

A word — remove overtones so it means fewer things, more strongly.

TUNE

Two words held together and pulled apart.

RUN VII

Opposition Design

Why you want the next turn

  • A rival who plays after you and takes overtones off the table.
  • A listener you must convince, who is bored by repetition — COLD twice pays less than COLD once.
  • The round DRIFTS — after each play the demand moves toward what you played, or away from it.
  • The table answers with a word and you must top it.
  • A diegetic clock — the candle, the thaw, the tide — rather than a play counter.
  • Something on the table that persists and grows: a poem, a structure, a creature.
  • Remove the score preview. Let the player be wrong, and let being wrong be interesting.
  • Fewer, heavier turns. Twelve rich decisions beat thirty-two thin ones.
  • One hand per round instead of four plays, so each play carries weight.
  • Make discarding cost something real. Four free discards is churn.
Insight — the cheapest large win

FATIGUE. An overtone you used goes dull for a few turns. One rule, and the same play twice stops working — which is the whole repetition complaint, answered cheaply.

Structure and Shareability

Synthesis

THE VERB IS WRONG

PICK ▸▸▸ JOIN

Pick is selection. Join is creation.

Overtone's verb is PICK — choose 3 of 7 against a checklist the game has already scored for you. Every symptom the players report follows from it: no skill expression, no composition, no surprise, no artifact, no reason to tell anyone about it.

The fix is not a better scoring curve. It is a different verb.

The game already contains its own answer — twice over

Insight

While the card prints its own overtones and the board prints the score before you commit, there is no judgement left to make and no way to be cleverer than the person next to you. Hide them and the player's own sense of English becomes the skill — which is exactly what “meaning is the physics” promised.

Insight

Written Lenses beat bought Lenses. A line you actually played, kept as a permanent effect and named after itself, gives the run an object that is yours, makes the Bookseller visible, and turns a score into something worth showing another person.
