---
topic: The figure ledger — figures the player completes are remembered for the run and pay more next time
---

- event: spec created from docs/brainstorming/brainstorm-overtone-core-loop-2026-09-17/brainstorm-intent.md (rich input, direct extraction, no elicitation)
- decision: slug is `figure-ledger`, inherited from the change rather than the input's shape
- constraint: vanilla JS, single file, no dependencies, no build step — inherited from the target codebase, non-negotiable
- decision: the ledger counts PLAYS OF THE FIGURE THAT SCORED, not figures merely present in the hand. resolve() already picks one best figure per hand and only that one pays; levelling the one that paid keeps scoring and levelling reading from the same fact.
- insight: that choice also solves the PAIR problem without a special case. PAIR is reachable in ~95% of hands, so a naive "every figure present levels" rule would max it immediately. PAIR only *scores* when nothing better was available, so it levels on the consolation track, which is where it belongs.
- decision: thresholds are triangular (1, 3, 6, 10 prior plays) rather than linear. A linear ladder capped at 4 would max out by play 4 of 32 and re-stagnate the rest of the run; triangular makes level 4 a genuine 10-play commitment out of 32.
- decision: chips scale to x2.4 across the ladder, multiplier adds at most +2 and not at all below level 3. Multiplier is the runaway term and was already priced against measured figure reachability (pair ~95% of plays, stair 87%, column 55%, chain-of-three 36%, monogram 8%).
- constraint: level 0 pays exactly what the game pays today. The first time a player meets any figure, nothing has changed — the ladder is additive on top of the shipped balance, never a nerf to it.
- constraint: figValue(f) = f.chips + 150 * f.mult is the single ranking function used by resolve(), the reorder nudge and the reference panel. All three must keep agreeing after the change, or the board will advise a play it does not then pay for.
- decision: acceptance bound on balance is SCHOLAR win rate over 400 simulated runs must land in [15%, 30%] and must not exceed 30%. Baseline is 23.5%. tools/balance.js verifies it.
- assumption: the ledger is a run-scoped counter on G and therefore is covered by whatever save/restore G already has; no new persistence layer.
- capability: CAP-1 the ledger accumulates — only the figure that scored increments
- capability: CAP-2 a levelled figure pays more — generous in points, sparing in multiplier
- capability: CAP-3 the ladder is capped and reachable — top at 10 prior plays of 32
- capability: CAP-4 the ledger is visible where the decision is made — on the board, not in a menu
- capability: CAP-5 the ledger is run-scoped — resets on new run, survives reload
- capability: CAP-6 the board never advises a play it will not pay for — resolve(), nudge and panel agree
- event: self-validate pass 1 (coherence) PASS. All six capabilities carry intent and success. Intents state WHAT; the ladder tables were routed to companion ledger-mechanics.md rather than into the kernel, per Spec Law rule 2. Five non-goals. Success signal is demonstrable (same seed, different shape commitment, materially different score) and measurable (simulator band).
- event: self-validate pass 2 (preservation) PASS. Every load-bearing claim in brainstorm-intent.md landed: the problem statement and the one-feedback-loop diagnosis in Why; the 23.5% measurement and its "do not retune the curve" consequence in Why and Non-goals; all five design constraints across CAP-1/3/4/5 and Constraints; the existing-surface bindings in Constraints and the companion; the deferred and rejected clusters in Non-goals.
- note: wrapper-only content deliberately not lifted — the three-persona rationale for WHY this change was chosen over the others. It justifies the selection but changes no downstream decision, so it stays in the brainstorm source rather than bloating the kernel.
- question: open — should a first-time figure pay a one-off discovery bonus (breadth vs the ladder's pull toward depth)? Shipping without it is coherent.
- question: open — level shown on the board as a number, a pip row, or both?
- decision: open question 1 resolved — NO discovery bonus this pass. The spec states shipping without it is coherent and adding it later is additive; keeping the change to one mechanic keeps it measurable against the 23.5% baseline.
- decision: open question 2 resolved — the level shows as a numeral beside the named figure during selection, and the run's ledger shows as a five-shape pip row on the board. Numeral where precision matters, pips where the shape is scanning for pattern.
- event: implementation found a defect in this spec, not in the code. CAP-5 asked that a reload preserve the ledger; the game has no mid-run persistence for anything (no saveRun, no G serialisation — start() calls newRun(null)), so a reload already discards round, score, deck and Lenses. The success criterion was unachievable and also inconsistent with the rest of the run.
- decision: CAP-5's reload clause dropped rather than a persistence layer invented for one counter. The assumption it rested on is struck through in SPEC.md with the correction recorded inline, so the spec carries its own errata.
- event: implementation also found a numeric fragility. Math.round(110 * 2.05) is 225, not 226 — three of the fifteen cells in the ladder land exactly on a half and the float lands just under it. FIG_CHIPS_X became FIG_CHIPS_PCT, integer percent divided at the end, which is exact for all fifteen. A scoring engine should not have cells whose value depends on binary representation.
- event: 400 runs on the first ladder (chips to 240%, mult +2) returned 29.0% against a 23.5% baseline. REJECTED, not accepted: the standard error at n=400 near 29% is 2.27 points, so the 95% interval is 24.6-33.4 and straddles the 30% bound. The point estimate cleared it; the evidence did not.
- insight: the simulator is a FLOOR. bestPlay() never takes a weaker hand now to level a shape for later, which is the exact strategy the ladder exists to enable, so a committed player beats the reported number. That pushes the true rate up, not down, which is the wrong direction to be uncertain in.
- decision: the constraint "chips generously, multiplier stingily" was itself wrong for this engine and is corrected in SPEC.md. It is true of a figure's own additive multiplier and false of the whole: a figure's chips are then multiplied by the entire deck, so at the x60 a run reaches by p90 the +448 chip bonus on a maxed MONOGRAM was worth 27,000 points. Neither term is safe when the multiplier is large.
- decision: ladder repriced to FIG_CHIPS_PCT [100,120,140,160,180] and FIG_MULT_UP [0,0,0,0,1]. Top bonus is +80% points and +1 multiplier, roughly half the first ladder. These steps also divide every FIG_PAY base exactly, so all 40 cells are integers and nothing rounds.
- event: the brainstorm keepsake was re-read against the implementation and found TWO parts of the chosen cluster unbuilt. Cluster A is "figure levelling, the discovery bonus, naming levels, showing the ledger on the board, breadth-vs-depth" - I had shipped levelling and the board display only.
- decision: levels are named rather than numbered. The session log said it plainly: "a COLUMN at level 3 is not COLUMN x3, it is the player's COLUMN, sharpened." I shipped LV3, which is exactly the counter form it argued against. NOTICED / PRACTISED / FLUENT / INSTINCT, a register that follows APPRENTICE and SCHOLAR.
- decision: the discovery bonus is in. FIG_FOUND = 150, flat, paid once per shape per run. Flat on purpose: it makes finding a STAIR (80 base) worth as much as finding a MONOGRAM (320), so the cheap shapes become worth one visit and breadth genuinely competes with depth every hand.
- decision: the bonus lives in resolve(), NOT in figPay(). Two reasons. figPay's level 0 must keep paying exactly the shipped value - that is a spec constraint with a test behind it - and folding a bonus into the ladder would break it. And it is a one-off term, which is what resolve() is for.
- decision: it is NOT a separate scoring event. A second label born on the figure's beat would sit ten pixels under the widest label the game prints, in the adjacent lane - the exact overlap that already had to be fixed once in this file. One label, one beat: "THE COLUMN · FIRST +300".
- event: measured. Ladder alone at n=1200 is 25.0% (CI 22.6-27.5), so the acceptance bound is MET for the ladder. Ladder plus discovery at n=400 is 26.5% (CI 22.2-30.8, crosses the bound on width alone). Because the simulator walks a fixed seed prefix, the 400 seeds are a strict subset of the 1200 - so this is a PAIRED comparison: ladder-only scored 25.3% on those same 400 seeds, and the bonus costs +1.2 points. Expected ~26.2% at n=1200. Confirmation running.
- event: ACCEPTANCE BOUND MET. 1200 runs, SCHOLAR, optimal play: 24.9% (95% CI 22.5-27.3) against a 23.5% baseline and a [15,30] bound. Deaths spread 0.2 / 1.9 / 11.0 / 13.7 / 19.8 / 13.6 / 15.0 across rounds 2-8. The ladder-reach alarm reads 1200 of 1200 runs levelling something, p50 level 2, p90 3, max 4 - so the ledger is being banked and used, and the number describes the game with it in.
- event: my own extrapolation was WRONG and it is worth writing down. From the 400-seed subset I measured the discovery bonus at +1.2 points and predicted ~26.2% at n=1200. Measured on the same 1200 seeds the bonus is -0.1 points: 25.0% without it, 24.9% with. The +1.2 was subset noise, not signal, and the paired-comparison argument I used to justify the extrapolation was sound in form and wrong in fact - pairing removes seed variance between two CONFIGURATIONS on the same seeds, it does not make 400 seeds representative of 1200. Third time this session a 400-run reading has misled.
- insight: a discovery bonus that costs nothing in win rate is the ideal outcome rather than a suspicious one. It is a counterweight, not a power-up: it pays for going wide, and going wide costs the ladder levels that paying for going deep would have earned. The two halves cancel in the aggregate and disagree on every single hand, which is exactly the decision the session asked for.
