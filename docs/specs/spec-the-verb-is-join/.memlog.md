topic: Overtone's core verb changes from PICK to JOIN - words score against each other rather than against a checklist
---

- event: create. Source is docs/brainstorming/brainstorm-overtone-make-it-fun-2026-09-17/.memlog.md, read in full. Mode: express - the user decided the direction and asked not to be interrogated.
- direction: The user authorised rebuilding the core loop from scratch. Lexicon, 19-tag ontology, card art, audio, daily seed, Demands and Lens concepts survive and are not re-authored. The scoring maths and the figure system are replaced.
- decision: The line is ORDERED and scoring reads ADJACENT PAIRS, not the set. Order was already meant to matter (the old figures read left to right) but nothing in the set-based scoring made it matter. Adjacency is what turns PICK into JOIN.
- decision: Three relations between two words, all read off tags already in the file. RESONANCE = shared overtones. TENSION = an opposed pair. SILENCE = neither. Measured on the real lexicon: 49.7% of all pairs resonate, 17.0% are in tension only, 33.2% are silent, 11.9% are both at once.
- decision: The opposition table is HEAT/COLD, DARK/BRIGHT, NATURE/TECH, MIND/BODY, WET/HEAT, and ABSTRACT against each of BODY, FOOD and TOOL. Eight pairs, all implied by the existing ontology, nothing newly authored.
- decision: A silent join STOPS the line. Words before it score, words after it do not. This is what makes order a real decision and length a real risk, without a fail state that wastes the turn entirely.
- assumption: Silence stopping the line rather than voiding it. Voiding was considered and rejected - a rule that can waste a whole turn on a guess, while overtones are hidden, punishes the exact exploration the design wants.
- decision: CAP-2's five figures are rhetorical and read only the tag matrix: ANTITHESIS (a join in tension), PARADOX (a join both resonant and in tension), SYNONYMY (a join sharing 3+), ESCALATION (shared count strictly rising across two joins), CHIASMUS (four words, joins of kind A-B-A).
- insight: JUXTAPOSITION was in the brainstorm as the surreal high-paying figure but cannot coexist with "silence stops the line". Dropped rather than special-cased. PARADOX replaces it as the rare, beautiful one and is better evidenced: 11.9% of pairs, and OWL/EYE is one.
- decision: Overtones are hidden on the card and learned across runs, stored in localStorage. This turns the 249-word lexicon from a thin content library into the progression, and makes the player's own sense of English the skill the game tests.
- assumption: Learning persists across runs rather than within one. A within-run version teaches nothing lasting; across runs the daily gives veterans an edge over newcomers, which the difficulty setting already exists to absorb.
- decision: FATIGUE. An overtone that scored goes dull for the next 2 plays and contributes nothing while dull. One rule against the "every turn is the same turn" complaint.
- decision: A line you played becomes a Lens you keep - it grants a standing bonus to the figure it made, named after the line itself. Replaces buying Lenses from a list. Slots stay at 5.
- decision: Fewer, heavier turns. 6 rounds x 2 plays = 12 plays per run, against 8 x 4 = 32 now. Line length 2 to 5 rather than exactly up to 3.
- decision: No score preview. The board shows the line and the relations it has found so far, never the total, until the player commits.
- constraint: All numeric constants in this spec are starting points. Balance is not asserted, it is measured - tools/balance.js is rebuilt for the new scoring and the acceptance bound in CAP-7 is what decides them.
- event: self-validate pass 1 (coherence) - PASS. Seven capabilities, each with intent and success. Constraints all rule something out. Four non-goals. Success signal is a measurable demonstration.
- event: self-validate pass 2 (preservation) - PASS. Every decision and insight in the source memlog's synthesis is carried. Source ideas not taken forward (the chain-to-a-destination game, the duet, word ageing, the librarian framing) are deliberately out of scope and named in Non-goals or left in the brainstorm, which remains readable.
- event: probed the proposed physics against 400 real dealt hands before writing the companion, rather than asserting the numbers.
- note: CAP-1 holds with room to spare. Best ordering of the same three words beats the worst by x6.00 at p50 (x1.62 at p25, x18.18 at p90) against a bound of +40%. Order is not decoration under these rules.
- note: CAP-3 holds. The best line in an opening hand makes 3 distinct figures at p50 and 4 at p90.
- insight: DESIGN DEFECT FOUND AND RESOLVED. An optimiser with full information hit a silence in 0.0% of 400 hands - a connected 5-word path essentially always exists when two thirds of all pairs connect. So the silence rule carries no risk on its own, and CAP-7's "a line stopped early" acceptance was unmeasurable as written.
- decision: The resolution is that silence is a risk BECAUSE the overtones are hidden, not because connected paths are rare. That moves CAP-4 from a flavour change to the load-bearing one: without hidden information CAP-2's silence rule is inert. The dependency order in the brainstorm was right for a reason it did not state.
- decision: The silence acceptance check moves from CAP-7 to CAP-4 and is measured against a player model that can only see the overtones of words it has already played. The full-information 0.0% is recorded as the contrast.
- note: Worked lines the rules actually produced, unedited: MIDNIGHT . LANTERN . SHADOW . SANDSTORM . LANDMINE (2,072); REED . EMBER . GLINT . SANDSTORM . FALCON (2,232); INSTINCT . BREATH . BANDWIDTH . AMBUSH . DAWN (1,220). These read as lines rather than as selections, which is the success signal.
- note: A five-word line pays 1,200 to 2,200 at round 1 under the starting constants, against a current round-1 target of 350. TARGETS is rescaled by the simulator, not by hand.
- event: steps 1 to 3 of migration.md built as tools/join-sim.js and measured. The design question is answered.
- note: CAP-7 MET. 500 runs, full information, TARGETS base 1.0x the round-1 ceiling and a 1.30 curve: 20.6% win rate, 95% CI 17.1 - 24.1, inside [15, 30]. Deaths spread 10.2 / 20.6 / 14.0 / 13.4 / 10.2 / 11.0 across all six rounds - a better spread than the shipped game manages.
- note: CAP-3 MET with room. 4 distinct figures per run at p50, 5 at p90, against a bound of 3.
- note: CAP-1 MET with room. Best ordering of three words beats the worst by x6.00 at p50.
- note: two simulator bugs were found and fixed before any of these numbers were trusted. The line search compared against best.total where best held {idx, score}, so every comparison after the first was against undefined and the search kept whichever line it happened to see first. And played cards were discarded rather than cycled, which ran a 26-card deck dry on the fifth play and reported a design failure that belonged to the simulation.
- insight: CAP-4 CANNOT BE SETTLED BY SIMULATION, and that is a fact about the capability rather than about the simulator. A player model with no semantic knowledge at all wins 0.7% and dies in round 1 three quarters of the time. But that model is not a beginner, it is somebody who does not speak English. The entire premise of hiding the overtones is that a human already knows FURNACE is hot and FROST is cold, and a simulator has no such prior to model. The true number is somewhere between 0.7% and 20.6% and only a human playtest can find it.
- decision: CAP-4's acceptance criterion as written is unmeasurable and is replaced. The build proceeds with the overtones hidden on SCHOLAR and shown on APPRENTICE, so that the difficulty setting carries the information asymmetry and a first-time player is never asked to guess blind. Whether SCHOLAR's hidden mode is playable is a question for a human, and it is named as the one risk this spec cannot retire on its own.
- decision: The constants are settled for the build. JOIN_RESONANCE 40, JOIN_TENSION 1, DEMAND_PTS 25, LINE 2-5, ROUNDS 6, PLAYS 2, FATIGUE_SPAN 2, KEPT_CHIPS 120, KEPT_MULT 1, LENS_SLOTS 5, TARGETS = round-1 ceiling x 1.30^r.
- event: the physics is in index.html and measured through the shipped engine rather than a standalone model.
- note: PORT VERIFIED. 30,240 lines scored through the game's own resolve() and through an independent re-implementation written from join-mechanics.md. Zero disagreements.
- note: CAP-7 MET IN THE GAME. 400 runs, SCHOLAR, optimal play: 22.0%, against the standalone model's 20.6% - the port did not drift. Runs end in every one of the six rounds (3.8 / 10.3 / 18.0 / 22.8 / 9.8, with 35.5% reaching round 6), so deaths are spread across six rather than the four CAP-7 asks for.
- note: CAP-3 MET IN THE GAME. Every one of 400 runs wrote a figure; 4 distinct figures at p50, 5 at p90.
- insight: A REAL BUG WAS FOUND BY THE REBUILD, not introduced by it. deckCeiling() walked every ordering of its pool to quote "your deck makes about X" in the shop. At three words and a nine-card pool that was the ~25ms its own comment claimed. At five words and a sixteen-card pool it is 571,456 resolve() calls per play - eleven seconds of frozen browser every time the Bookseller opened, and 21 seconds per simulated run. Replaced with a beam search keeping the best 24 prefixes at each length: 116ms per run, a 180x speedup, and the shop opens instantly.
- decision: lineWorth() is now the one cheap ranking function, in index.html and exported, used by deckCeiling's beam and by balance.js's shortlist. Two copies of "roughly what is this line worth" would have drifted apart the first time a constant moved.
- decision: figureByReorder() looks past lines of four. At five words it is 120 permutations on every click, and the nudge is not worth that.
- event: first human playtest. The verdict was "this game is too hard to understand", and it was not a comprehension problem - it was a defect I shipped.
- insight: CAP-4 TIED HIDING TO SCHOLAR, AND THAT WAS WRONG IN A WAY NO SIMULATION COULD HAVE CAUGHT. Anybody who had played the game before already had SCHOLAR selected and the tutorial marked seen, and the learned store did not exist until the day it shipped. So a returning player opened it to seven blank cards, no tutorial to explain them, and a coach line reading "Gold tags are what this round wants" - pointing at gold tags that had just been made invisible. The game gave an instruction it had made impossible to follow. The spec said "APPRENTICE shows everything, so nobody meets this blind" and the returning-SCHOLAR path was never checked.
- decision: Hiding becomes an opt-in preference, default off, with its own switch and a sentence saying what it does. It stops being a side effect of difficulty. This is the better design regardless: it is a mode a player chooses rather than something that happens to them, and it can now be tested on its own.
- note: the coach line is computed rather than fixed, so it can never again name something the board is not drawing.

- event: CONSTRAINT WITHDRAWN. "The board never shows the score of an uncommitted line" shipped and was read as a broken counter. The reasoning stands and the reading was still correct; the counters follow the selection again, and the relation beads keep the half of it that was teaching something.
- decision: ASSUMPTION REVISED. 6 rounds of 3 plays, not 2. Two leaves the round no middle and no turn that hinges on what the first play spent - the turn CAP-5 exists for. TARGETS re-derived by sweep, both curves re-measured.
- direction: Player asked for the remaining test failures and defects to be cleared.
- event: TEST BACKLOG CLOSED. The whole of it was assertions written against the pre-JOIN game, kept out of scope since the rebuild. Node 165/165, E2E 370 passed / 0 failed / 8 skipped across desktop and phone at --retries=0.
- decision: tests/figures.test.js REWRITTEN rather than repaired. Every test in it was about THE PAIR, THE COLUMN, THE STAIR, THE MONOGRAM and THE CHAIN - figures that read the letters of a word, which CAP-1 deleted. The concerns survived the rebuild even though the answers did not, so the new file asks the same questions of the JOIN hands: what a given run of joins makes, that order decides it, that every hand a line makes is paid and not only the best, that the hands land before the Lenses, and that the reorder nudge never invents one.
- note: The old file's proportional-Demand tests went with it. A figure used to pay its multiplier only in proportion to how many of its words the round wanted; under JOIN the Demand pays per card and the hands pay outright, so there was nothing left to test.
- note: DEFECT CLASS, eight tests in lenses.test.js and one in logic.test.js. Their fixtures were two or three words with nothing in common - fine when scoring read a bag of cards, fatal now that a line stops at the first pair that shares nothing. CARNIVORE's ANIMAL word, THE ORACLE's silenced word and THE FAMINE's second word were never reached, so each Lens was measured as paying zero and the tests were passing or failing for reasons unrelated to the Lens. Every fixture now carries a shared overtone; compare() scores the line with and without the Lens, so the join cancels out of the difference.
- note: Three assertions in the same file were reading the HAND's multiplier where they meant the LENS's. An opposition is worth multiplier in its own right, so ANTONYM ENGINE's fixture no longer starts at x1.
